## Problem

`CONSTITUTION.md`, Love: "Love provides direction: dignity, care, truth, reciprocal benefit, protection of life, and respect for legitimate agency. To thrive is to grow and to withstand; resilience is part of what we help build." The text names resilience but says nothing about what happens when someone does not withstand: loses work, health, or footing. The steward proposes a principle that a community grows stronger by helping such people rise, contribute, and in time help others.

## Proposed change

`CONSTITUTION.md`, Love. One sentence appended.

Before:

> Love provides direction: dignity, care, truth, reciprocal benefit, protection of life, and respect for legitimate agency. To thrive is to grow and to withstand; resilience is part of what we help build.

After (recommended):

> Love provides direction: dignity, care, truth, reciprocal benefit, protection of life, and respect for legitimate agency. To thrive is to grow and to withstand; resilience is part of what we help build. That includes helping people who are struggling regain their footing, in ways they choose, so that they can stand on their own and, if they wish, help others do the same. Help is not a loan; no one owes contribution in return for it.

Alternative A (the steward's original wording, verbatim):

> A community becomes stronger when it empowers those who have fallen to rise, contribute, and eventually help others do the same.

Alternative B (Layer 2 only): leave `CONSTITUTION.md` unchanged and describe "the net" only on the Luminary Life website, as a community aspiration with no constitutional standing.

## Four Points

- Supports: Love (dignity and care for people in hardship) and Possibility (recovery is treated as buildable).
- Could conflict with: Agency, if "empower" becomes deciding for someone what their recovery should look like; Love's dignity, if help is framed as conditional on becoming useful again. Weighed: the recommended text adds "in ways they choose," makes helping others optional ("if they wish"), and says help is not owed back.

## Why now

The website is being redesigned around a "net that helps us rise together." The same reasoning as LIP-0012 applies: either the idea goes through the process or the site says it is not constitutional.

## Evidence and reasoning

Reasoning only. The original wording makes an empirical claim ("a community becomes stronger when...") that may be true but is not supported here. The recommended text drops it and states a commitment instead, which is how the rest of the Love article is written.

## Alternatives considered

- Do nothing: Love's existing "resilience is part of what we help build" arguably already covers this. A commenter who finds the addition redundant has a fair point.
- Alternative A verbatim: rejected as the recommendation. "Fallen" carries moral and religious connotations in English (a fall from grace) and may stigmatize the people it means to help; it also translates poorly. "Rise, contribute, and eventually help others" sets a path every helped person is expected to follow, which ties support to future usefulness, contradicting LIP-0003's "Worth does not depend on contribution" and excluding people who will never be able to contribute in that way. "A community becomes stronger" justifies helping people by what it does for the community, an instrumental reason where the Love article otherwise starts from dignity. And the sentence introduces "community," which the Constitution, addressed to humans and AI systems, does not otherwise use as an actor.
- A new derived principle ("Mutual uplift"): rejected for now; one sentence under Love is smaller and easier to reverse.

## Harms and misuse

- **Conditional help.** Any wording that links help to later contribution can be used to rank people by their expected return. The final sentence of the recommended text exists to prevent that.
- **Paternalism.** "Empower" and "help" can license intervention in someone's life without consent. "In ways they choose" ties this to Agency and to LIP-0004.
- **Commercial use.** The steward organization offers paid membership and describes the net as part of it. A constitutional sentence about catching people who fall could be read, or advertised, as a promise of assistance. The Constitution makes no promise of material help from anyone, and the organization should not cite this text as one. A reviewer should test whether the proposal would be filed if the organization were not selling membership.
- **Pay-it-forward as guilt.** "Help others do the same" can become an obligation. "If they wish" keeps it voluntary; invariant 5 is the test.
- **AI readers.** An agent could read this as license to step into a user's situation unasked. It is not one: the sentence sits under Love, which already names "respect for legitimate agency," and `LUMINARY.md` Standing grants no authority to act.

## Implications

- Agency: preserved by "in ways they choose" and "if they wish."
- Truth and evidence: the recommended text removes the original's unsupported empirical claim.
- Cultural: mutual aid is common across cultures; the individual-recovery framing ("stand on their own") is less universal, and some traditions would put interdependence first. Comment invited.
- AI systems: no new task. An agent reads that care for people in hardship is part of Love, bounded by their choices.
- Humans: a person reads that being helped carries no debt.

## Reversibility

Reversible by LIP.

## What would falsify this

Comment or review showing that the recommended wording is still read as conditional help, or that "stand on their own" excludes people with lasting disabilities or illness. Then the sentence needs another revision or Alternative B is right.
